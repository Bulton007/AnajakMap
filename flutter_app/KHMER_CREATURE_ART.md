# Khmer-inspired fantasy companions

The six original companions use distinct serpent, guardian-cub, bird, water-chimera,
terrapin and monkey silhouettes in vector portraits and procedural 3D meshes.
Lotus shapes, flowing ornament, curling tails and warm metal accents provide a
shared visual language. Their game names, colours, powers and biographies are
fiction. They are not historical reconstructions or depictions of named deities.
The terrapin is an original nature companion, not a claimed traditional myth.

Stable species IDs, encounter weights, statistics, elements, grades and saved
collections remain unchanged. The English display names are shared with the
backend; Khmer names and short fictional lore come from `creature_catalog.dart`.
All artwork is code-native original work; no photographs or third-party art were
copied. The portrait renderer and 3D renderer both use the same six design families.

Primary sources consulted for the broad visual motifs (not for game lore):

- [Cambodian Ministry of Culture and Fine Arts: Naga balustrade conservation](https://mcfa.gov.kh/apsara-national-authority-restores-20-meter-west-corner-naga-balustrade/).
- [Tokyo National Museum: Khmer sculpture, including Garuda riding on Naga](https://www.tnm.jp/modules/r_exhibition/index.php?controller=item&id=6883&lang=en).
- [UNESCO: Lkhon Khol Wat Svay Andet photographic record](https://ich.unesco.org/en/10a-urgent-safeguarding-list-01012?id=01374&include=slideshow_inc.php), including a monkey-general motif from the Reamker tradition.

These references informed the design direction; museum objects, names of named
sacred figures and ceremonial performances are not presented as game enemies.
